# Several firms per commodity: results

Design in `docs/130`. Sweep: `k = 4`, `DEMAND = 800`, 20 seeds, committed
as `economy_lab/battery130_results.json`. The one-seed 180-day readings
quoted for predictions 2 and 3 come from the same configuration.

Five passes, two failures, both low, and the two failures are the
substance.

### Prediction 4: one price, differential profit, equal size

The sector's producers end up the **same size to six decimal places**.
Traced on one seed: the within-sector coefficient of variation of the
*posted price* is 3.6e-6 — the posted-price rule pulls a sector to one
price, exactly as `docs/58` §5a reported for two provisioners, and the
share rule weights by posted price, so equal prices give equal shares.
The 10% overhead spread does not vanish; it goes where classical theory
says it goes. Within sectors the Spearman correlation between overhead
rank and *distributed profit* rank is **−0.84** (n = 865). Cheaper
producers earn more, sell the same, and stay the same size.

That is the finding of this document. **Our lab's competition equalises
prices and differentiates profit rates; Wright's differentiates size.**
His firm-size tail is generated by a lottery that lets revenue vary
independently of cost, and by hiring that follows revenue. Ours has no
path from a cost advantage to a size advantage, because nothing a
producer earns changes what it is allotted tomorrow. The design said
that if 4 failed *high* there would be a mechanism worth its own
document; it failed low, and the mechanism that is absent is named.

Prediction 3's sign flips on some seeds (range up to +0.57) are the same
fact seen through noise: with size differences of 1e-6, rank is decided
by rounding, and the median of −0.55 is the ordering the elasticity
imposes at the sixth decimal.

### Prediction 6: demises are per commodity, not per firm

I predicted more deaths from smaller firms sitting nearer the quantum.
The demise rate *fell* to 0.0029 from 0.0099. The reason is that
`DEMAND` was scaled with `k` precisely so that firm size did not
change, so no firm sits nearer the quantum than before; the deaths are
still the ~26 commodities whose sectors cannot reach one worker at any
`k`, now four firms each, over a denominator four times larger. Deaths
scale with commodities, the denominator with firms, and the rate goes as
`1/k`. Predictable from the design's own arithmetic, and not predicted.

### Not predicted: GDP kurtosis

GDP growth excess kurtosis reads **4.75** (range −1.5 to 9.1) against
`docs/129`'s −0.54 at `k = 1`. Not a prediction, so not a result; noted
because it moved, and because a fatter-tailed GDP series from more
firms on the same constant demand would need explaining before anyone
quotes it. Most likely it is the 55 deaths per run landing on a series
of 24 points.

## 2. What this leaves

The three scenario facts `docs/127` §4 listed have now each been given a
document. Two are resolved and one is measured:

- One producer per commodity → `producers_per_commodity` (this
  document). Resolved as a scenario limit; **the model's competition
  does not generate a size distribution from it**, and would not at any
  `k`.
- Prices that do not see the wage → they do; the wage is a price
  (`docs/128`). Resolved.
- Final demand a constant → **unchanged.** GDP growth and firm growth
  in Wright's sense both need demand that comes from the wages and
  profits the economy itself pays out, and a hiring rule that follows
  revenue. Neither exists in our lab and neither is a scenario knob.

That is where this line of work stops without a decision. Everything
from `docs/126` to here has been the scenario failing the model; what
remains is the model lacking two rules Wright has — **endogenous demand**
and **revenue-following employment** — and adding either changes what
the laboratory is. Both would be gated and bit-identical when off, as
every rule since `docs/55` has been, but they are additions to the
ontology, not calibrations of it, and `docs/117` §4 already put the
RimWorld mod ahead of further docking work. The choice between the two
is not one this document should make.

## 3. Method note

Prediction 6 was wrong by the design's own arithmetic: I scaled demand
so firm size would not change and then predicted a consequence of firm
size changing. `docs/125` §5's rule was about searching the record;
this one is closer — **check each prediction against the design's own
choices** before writing it. The two sentences were four paragraphs
apart.
