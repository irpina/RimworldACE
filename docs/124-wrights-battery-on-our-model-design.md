# Wright's battery on our own model: design

Written before anything is built or run. Measured in `docs/125`.

Three tasks, in dependency order: fix the estimator question (`§4`), then
run Wright's measures on our model with his own model as the control
(`§5`). All bands here are **two-sided**, which is `docs/120` §1's lesson
paid forward.

## 1. Why

`docs/111`–`docs/123` established that our *transcription* of Wright is
sound. It did not test our own laboratory at all. The docking was
supposed to answer "is our work sound", and so far it has only answered
"is our copy of his work sound".

The question this asks: **our lab has commodities, techniques, prices,
labour values, rent and two regions; his has actors and money. Does the
extra apparatus still produce capitalism's statistical signature, or does
it distort it?**

## 2. The ontology gap, stated before it embarrasses us

Wright's seven facts are not all measurable on our model, and one is not
measurable **in principle**:

| Wright's fact | our analogue | measurable? |
|---|---|---|
| firm size (employees) | producer worker-days ÷ quantum | yes |
| firm growth | change in producer employment | yes |
| GDP growth | regional gross product | yes |
| firm demises | bankruptcies + exits | yes, needs `producer_cash` |
| recession duration | from the GDP series | yes |
| rate of profit | `value_rate_of_profit` | yes |
| **income distribution** | — | **no** |
| wage share (his 0.55) | the `docs/99` identity | yes |

**There are no individuals in our model.** Labour is a continuous
regional pool with a wage rate, so there is nothing to have an income and
fact 6 cannot be attempted. Any document claiming we reproduced it would
be wrong.

## 3. The configuration, and why each part of it

Measured during design, not assumed:

- **`synthetic_economy(commodities=200, regions=2)`.** The hand-calibrated
  scenario has **6 producers per region** — no distribution to fit. The
  synthetic one has hundreds. This is the only size at which the question
  is askable.
- **Finite labour pools** (`labor_pool = 400` per region). The synthetic
  economy ships `float("inf")`, which means no reserve army and no
  unemployment, and Wright's unemployment is one of the facts.
- **`producer_cash=True, posted_prices=True, producer_employment_quantum=1.0`.**
  The quantum is what makes "firm size in whole workers" meaningful, and
  it is reachable **only inside the `producer_cash` branch** — measured:
  `producer_employment_quantum=1.0` without it is bit-identical to bare.
  So firm deaths and integer firm sizes come together or not at all.
- **Regional cash seeded ×10.** At the shipped endowment the cash rules
  fail on about day 75 with `cannot cover its wage bill`. At ×10 the
  economy runs 180 days with 528 active producers. This is a **scenario
  calibration choice and not a model change**; the failure is the model
  refusing to silently stop paying workers, which is correct behaviour.

## 4. Task one: Wright's own estimator, made first-class

`docs/123` §3 found that his α = 1.038 comes from *"an ordinary least
squares regression"* on a *"histogram … with a constant bin size of 1"*,
which is not our MLE, and that on our data his method returns anything
from 0.54 to 0.88 depending on which sparse bins are kept. That left the
central question open: **is the gap his estimator, or our transcription?**

Build `ols_histogram_fit(values, min_count=1)` in `distributions.py`,
returning α in Wright's convention, and characterise it against planted
data of **known** exponent. That converts the open question into a
calibration: if the estimator maps a known true α to a reported α, we can
invert Wright's 1.038.

## 5. Task two: the battery, with his model as the control

One measurement module runs the same estimators over both models, so a
difference cannot be an artefact of different measurement code. Facts are
scored per seed where n permits (`docs/118` §3's lesson), pooled where the
series is annual.

For firm size the key diagnostic is **not** the exponent. Our producers
do not grow by winning a lottery; capacity descends from authored final
demand through a Leontief cascade — measured at design time, 400
producers carry only **101 distinct capacity values**. So we report the
rank correlation between realised employment and authored
`capacity_per_day` alongside any fitted exponent, because a tail
inherited from the input-output graph is not the same finding as a tail
generated by firm dynamics.

## 6. Numbered predictions

Measured in `docs/125`.

**Estimator (task one).**

1. On planted continuous power-law data (true α_Wright = 1.5, n = 50,000),
   **MLE recovers it within 0.05 and OLS-all-bins is low by more than
   0.3.** Direction and magnitude both asserted.
2. **The OLS bias is not a small-sample effect**: its value at
   n = 100,000 is within 0.10 of its value at n = 10,000. If it shrinks
   toward zero the bias is sampling noise and point 2 of the memo in
   `docs/121` is wrong.
3. **Raising `min_count` monotonically reduces the bias**, because the
   bias is sparse tail bins: α at `min_count=10` is closer to truth than
   at `min_count=1`, for every n tested.
4. **Inverting the calibration on Wright's 1.038 lands above our measured
   1.278**, meaning his firm-size distribution is *thinner*-tailed than
   our transcription's. If instead it lands within 0.15 of 1.278,
   `docs/123` §3 resolves the other way — his number and ours agree once
   the estimator is accounted for, and the transcription is vindicated.

**Battery (task two).**

5. **The wage share transfers and the distributions do not.** Our wage
   share lands inside **[0.45, 0.65]**, Wright's own range, while firm
   growth excess kurtosis lands **outside [1.5, 4.5]** — the two-sided
   Laplace band from `docs/120`. This is the central claim: an accounting
   identity is ontology-independent, a distributional signature is not.
6. **Firm size in our model is largely authored.** Spearman correlation
   between realised employment and authored `capacity_per_day` exceeds
   **0.9**. Whatever exponent we fit is then a property of the
   input-output graph.
7. **Demises are far rarer than Wright's.** He loses ~18 firms a month
   from ~100, about 18% monthly. Ours is below **5%** of active
   producers per equivalent period, because our producers meet demand
   rather than gamble on a market lottery.
8. **The control reproduces.** Running the identical battery code on the
   SA model at β = 1 returns `docs/119`'s figures — α within 0.10 of
   1.296 and growth kurtosis within 0.5 of 3.889. A failure here means
   the battery is broken and every reading in `docs/125` is void.
9. **Money and labour conservation hold throughout**, and
   `python -m economy_lab.baseline` still shows 322 metrics at zero
   delta. The battery must not perturb the default model.

Predictions 5 and 8 are the substance. 5 is the finding; 8 is what makes
5 believable.

## 7. What this cannot conclude

It cannot show our model is *right*. Reproducing or failing to reproduce
Wright's signature is a statement about two models, and `docs/117` §7's
caution stands undiminished: neither his model matching stylized facts
nor ours matching his carries evidential weight about real economies.

It cannot rescue the firm-size power law. `docs/114` stands.

And prediction 6, if it holds, means our firm-size reading is close to
circular — we would be measuring the scenario author's choices. That is
a limit of the instrument, not a result, and `docs/125` must say so
rather than reporting an exponent as though it were emergent.
