# The inequality was an artefact of my own band

Corrects `docs/119` §6. No new runs; this re-reads the sweep already
recorded, plus the β ∈ {2, 3, 5} extension.

## 1. What I got wrong

`docs/119` concluded that Wright's seven facts constrain the size
exponent to **β ≥ 1** — an inequality — because six of seven still held
at β = 5. That conclusion came from a band I wrote in `docs/118` §4:

| fact | target | band I registered |
|---|---|---|
| firm-growth excess kurtosis | **Laplace = 3** | **≥ 2.0** |

I wrote the target and the band on the same line and did not notice they
disagree. Laplace is a **point**, not a floor. A floor cannot fail from
above, so a one-sided band guaranteed a one-sided region, and the "the
facts are indifferent above β = 1" finding was manufactured by the test
rather than measured.

This was visible without any data. It is not a case of the model
surprising me.

## 2. Firm-growth kurtosis discriminates sharply, and it binds from above

Per-seed medians with 95% intervals from 2000 bootstrap resamples over
seeds. Adjacent β do **not** overlap:

| β | 0.90 | 0.95 | **1.00** | 1.05 | 1.10 | 1.50 | 5.00 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| kurtosis | 1.540 | 2.286 | **3.889** | 5.596 | 6.530 | 9.076 | 6.384 |
| 95% | [1.50, 1.59] | [2.20, 2.41] | **[3.79, 4.22]** | [5.31, 5.89] | [6.26, 6.97] | [8.21, 9.52] | [6.16, 6.75] |

Laplace is 3. Above β = 1 the growth distribution is not "still heavy
enough" — it is **twice too heavy and getting heavier**. Read against a
two-sided target, every β ≥ 1.05 is rejected decisively.

So `docs/119` §4's "six of seven facts still hold at β = 5" is wrong.
Under a proper Laplace test it is five of seven, and the failure is on
one of the two facts Wright leads with.

## 3. The saturation finding survives; its interpretation does not

`docs/119` §5 stands as measured: the `drawn > self.market` clamp fires
0.0% of the time at β ≤ 1.0 and 99.0% at β = 5, and it pulls the fitted
**size exponent** back toward 1 (α = 1.053 at β = 5). That is real.

What does not stand is the inference that the model therefore cannot
tell β = 1 from β = 5. It can. Saturation rescues α and **does not
rescue growth kurtosis**, which stays at 6.4 where Laplace wants 3. The
clamp restores the *cross-sectional* fact and not the *dynamic* one.

## 4. The sharper result, which needs no bands at all

Drop the bands and score against Wright's two reported firm-level
numbers directly — α = 1.038 and Laplace's kurtosis of 3:

- The β at which **α matches 1.038** is **1.078**. Growth kurtosis there
  is **6.12**, more than double Laplace.
- The β at which **growth is actually Laplace** is **0.972**. α there is
  **1.505**, 45% above what he reports.
- **The two facts want exponents 0.106 apart.**

Minimising the relative squared error against both at once:

| β | α | kurtosis | joint loss |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.90 | 2.007 | 1.54 | 1.107 |
| 0.95 | 1.672 | 2.29 | 0.430 |
| **1.00** | **1.296** | **3.89** | **0.150** |
| 1.05 | 1.094 | 5.60 | 0.752 |
| 1.10 | 0.993 | 6.53 | 1.387 |
| 1.50 | 0.885 | 9.08 | 4.123 |

**The optimum is β = 1.00 exactly**, and the loss roughly triples for a
step of 0.05 in either direction.

## 5. What this means

**β = 1 is identified, sharply, and it is the paper's own value.** The
knife-edge reading of `docs/117` §3 is the correct one and `docs/119`'s
one-sided reading is withdrawn. Wright's setting is not a convention he
could have chosen otherwise; it is the joint optimum of his own facts.

**But it is a compromise, not a fit.** At β = 1 *neither* target is met:
α is 1.296 against 1.038, and kurtosis is 3.889 against 3. β = 1 wins
because it minimises a disagreement, not because it satisfies anything.

**That disagreement is a new, specific finding.** The firm-size exponent
and Laplace firm growth — the two results the paper leads with — are
generated by the same ingredient (`docs/116`) but want different
strengths of it. A single-setting model cannot show this; it is visible
only once the rule is continuous. Whether it indicates a missing
mechanism or is simply the residual of a deliberately minimal model is
not something this sweep can say.

**And the identification rests on one fact.** Of the seven, four are
indifferent to β across the whole range (wage share, recession λ, income
α, and unemployment as a diagnostic), one holds from 0.7 up (demises),
one from 1.0 up (α), and one is unresolvable (GDP kurtosis, `docs/119`
§3). **Firm-growth kurtosis alone supplies the upper constraint.** It is
also, by `docs/116`, the fact Wright attributes to contingency when it is
carried by linearity — so the one fact that pins the law of value's
exponent is the one his paper does not credit to the law of value.

## 6. Status of the bands

The two-sided reading in §2 uses a band chosen after seeing the data, and
I am not presenting it as pre-registered. §4 is the load-bearing
argument precisely because it uses **no bands** — only his two reported
numbers and a relative loss — and it reaches the same place.

`docs/118`'s pre-registered bands did their job in one respect: because
they were written down, the error in §1 is attributable to a specific
line rather than to hindsight. The rule to add:

> **A band must be able to fail in the direction the target can fail in.
> If the target is a point, a one-sided band cannot test it.**
