## 2. Sweep A: the fund is the reserve

The refusal at ×0.1 is not payroll. `_seed_purses` gives every producer
its working capital *out of the fund* and at ×0.1 the fund is emptied
before the first day, so the spending rate is undefined. At ×0.3 the
fund is left with 14% of the money stock after seeding, GDP growth std
reaches 0.18, and the population is the healthiest of the sweep (727
employing, 170 deaths). The stock-to-flow ratio does what `docs/133`
§3 said it would; a smaller fund follows distributions more closely and
demand moves. One seed, and ×10 → ×3 inverts by 0.002, which is the
seed, not the parameter — `docs/133`'s twenty-seed median at ×10 was
0.011 against this seed's 0.032.

## 3. Sweep B: dispersion is bounded, and the fit is not a fit

Sector CV rises with the spread exactly as predicted, 0.02 → 0.22, and
on the one seed traced it rises 0.012 → 0.060 → 0.198 over the first
twelve months and then **stops**: 0.165, 0.196, 0.183 at months 18, 24,
30, while employment falls from 861 to 505 and the largest firm from
2,700 to 1,300. Drift compounds until the over-accumulation attrition
of `docs/133` §3 begins, and then the sector is reshaped by deaths, not
by growth. Prediction 4 passes on its ratio and the compounding it was
written to detect is not there.

Prediction 5's first clause failed in a way that needs saying plainly.
Across five seeds the MLE's `x_min` is 420–870 on a largest firm of
2,400–3,000, so the "tail" it fits is the top factor of four of a
distribution whose top factor of four is the authored capacity spread
of the input-output graph (`docs/124` §5: 101 distinct capacity values).
The span across seeds is small because every seed has the same graph.
The fitted α runs 1.7–4.1 within a cell. This is `docs/119` §2's rule
— a fit over under two octaves is uninformative — and a small span
across seeds does not rescue it. The prediction passes on kurtosis; the
question it was written for is answered by §5.

## 4. Sweep C: the construction unit governs, not the retention

Total wage share falls from 0.75 to 0.18 across the grid and the
production share holds at 0.013 throughout, so construction can be
made a plausible share and the wage basis is untouched (prediction 6).
But the crisis did not ease where predicted. Ranked by deaths:

| retention × unit | deaths | employing |
|---|---|---|
| **0.5 × 20** | **84** | **932** |
| 0.2 × 20 | 145 | 879 |
| 0.05 × 20 | 230 | 670 |
| 0.2 × 60 | 315 | 567 |
| 0.05 × 60 | 316 | 550 |
| 0.5 × 60 | 375 | 534 |

At a cheaper construction unit, *more* retention means *fewer* deaths.
The unit sets the daily wear bill (`capacity × unit / life`), so
halving it thrice is what stops attrition; and given cheap capacity, a
firm that retains more grows its share of the order book, which is
allotted by capacity (`docs/131`), and pays its wear. Retention starves
the fund, not the firm. I predicted the opposite because I read
retention as the cause of over-accumulation and it is the *unit* that
decides whether accumulated capacity can be maintained. Prediction 7
fails, and the cell it named is the wrong one; 0.5 × 20 is the
configuration a further battery should run.

## 5. What this leaves

The standing question of `docs/133` §4 has its answer, and it is the
one predicted: **cost heterogeneity gives a deterministic lab a bounded
dispersion of firm sizes** — sector CV of about 0.2 at a spread of 1.0,
plateauing when attrition starts — **and no tail.** Wright's tail is
generated by revenue that varies independently of cost, every month,
for every firm; ours has a 3–30% drift difference that compounds for a
year and then meets its own over-capacity. His lottery is
load-bearing.

That leaves a decision that is not a sweep:

- **Add a stochastic market rule to our lab** — M1, or a smaller cousin
  of it — so that revenue has a noise term. It would be gated and
  bit-identical off like everything since `docs/55`, but it ends the
  laboratory's founding determinism inside the rule, and every
  reproducibility argument from `docs/32` onward would need the seed
  as an input. It is the only route to his firm-size and firm-growth
  facts that the last ten documents have not closed.
- **Or dock on what a deterministic lab can share with him** — money
  conservation, the wage share as an input, the cost-ranked sector, an
  endogenous over-accumulation crisis, GDP that moves when the fund is
  small — and write the memo to Wright on those terms, with the tail
  named as the fact our ontology does not produce and the reason.

The mod does not depend on this choice; the mod's economy is
deterministic by the game's own requirements and would take the second
path regardless. What depends on it is whether the docking is called
complete.

## 6. Method note

Prediction 7 named the wrong cell because I had a causal story
(retention → over-accumulation → deaths) and did not check it against
the mechanism that actually kills (unpaid wear ∝ unit). The rule from
`docs/133` §5 was to read the parameters a rule multiplies; this one is
to **read which parameter the failure mode multiplies**, which is not
always the one the rule is named for.
